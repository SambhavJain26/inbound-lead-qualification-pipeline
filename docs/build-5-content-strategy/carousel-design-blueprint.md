# Carousel content and design blueprint

Updated 2026-09-17 after the second design review. This is the current production
brief for six carousels, not a claim that new PDFs have been rendered or approved.
The current exact public copy is in [linkedin-post-scripts.md](linkedin-post-scripts.md).
Earlier seven-page PDF/PNG proofs are rejected and superseded. The first ten-page
proof is now rendered and inspected. The user requested the next deck, so the
nine-page B5-04 review-file checklist is also rendered and inspected; the other
four remain briefs. Individual technical QA does not establish design approval.
See the first and B5-04 self-assessments.

## 1. What these documents must do for the reader

Carousels are the highest-value teaching format in this content package. A reader
should leave with a method, a check, an example or a worksheet they can use in
an operations conversation. A sequence of opinions is not enough.

Every teaching page needs one useful instruction and enough context to apply it.
The deck must stand on its own: the caption can explain scope, but cannot rescue
missing steps or turn an empty slogan into a useful method.

## 2. Copy balance and value test

Teaching copy generally sits around 20–35 title/body words. Current pages range
26–35; they retain practical checks instead of shrinking to the rejected 8–13-word
statements. Word count excludes the footer. Don't treat the range as a legal limit
or pad a useful page to reach it.

The final pages supply the actual field names or inspection test. They are not
advertisements for an unavailable download. The income deck includes two labeled
illustrative examples; those figures aren't real applicants or stored demo results.

Before a page ships, answer:

- What can the reader do after reading this page?
- What context would prevent them from applying the instruction incorrectly?
- What evidence, owner, boundary or next action does it identify?
- Is this teaching a new point, or repeating the previous page?
- Would it still make sense if someone saved the PDF without the caption?

Do not add legal thresholds, applicant judgments, client results or a provider
recommendation to make a deck sound valuable. The useful insight is the review
method, not a fabricated accuracy or time-saving claim.

## 3. Why the two previous designs failed

The first proof averaged 28.1 main-copy words per page but repeated a large title
and explanatory paragraph. The sparse revision averaged 10 and lost instructional
value. Its annotations leaned on gold highlights; tiny blue page numbers did not
provide a meaningful counterweight.

The answer is not another arbitrary percentage reduction. This brief keeps useful
information, uses a teaching-page hierarchy and assigns each annotation a job.
The new copy is not a midpoint calculation between the rejected versions.

## 4. What carries over from the vendor references

Use the vendor COI marked-document vocabulary: cream paper, navy ink, Switzer,
gold marker, blue pen, selection boxes and one dark emphasis page. Keep the shared
frame and grid; vary composition only when it helps the content.

Borrow the field/list/contrast rhythm, not vendor subject matter or supplier
performance claims. Reference files remain read-only. The maintenance kit is the
style foundation, not a reason to obey an old cover-only box rule after the user
asked for fuller use of blue.

## 5. Palette and annotation roles

| Element | Color | Meaning and use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper | Existing cream #FDF0D6 | Calm document ground, not an excuse to flood copy with gold. |
| Main type | Navy #02081F | Default reading color; do not randomly color individual words. |
| Blue selection box | #1F4393 with cream text | A central record, distinction or reusable field. Use selectively, including interiors when useful. |
| Blue underline | #3563CA | A check, requirement or responsibility the reader should retain. |
| Blue circle | #3563CA | A state, source or ambiguous term that deserves inspection. |
| Gold highlight | #F8C043 with navy type | A selected phrase carrying the lesson, not every heading. |
| Dark emphasis page | Navy #02081F | One authority or completion boundary; cream type and gold pen for contrast. |

Balance is visual, not equal mark counts. A few filled blue boxes can carry more
area than several pen underlines. Long gold swipes can outweigh many blue page numbers.
Review filled area, line weight and where the eye lands in the actual render.

Across the deck, alternate emphasis according to the content: an underline on
one page, a highlight on another, a circle on a comparison and a box on a key field.
Do not prescribe a blue-and-gold pair on every page. Ink-only quiet pages are useful
too. Don't put four devices on a page merely because the kit supports them.

Use zero, one or two focal annotations per page. The mix belongs to the whole
carousel, not a checklist for each page. Page furniture must not be counted as proof
that the palette is balanced. The inventory below describes a starting plan, not
a device quota; change it when an actual render or the content calls for restraint.
Keep the blue page numbers without treating them as substantive annotations. For the next proof, change the eyebrow's short rule to
blue on cream pages and gold on the dark page; retain the gold byline rule.

Counts describe the copy's planned annotation types. On the dark page, any pen
stroke becomes gold for legibility; it must not falsely be counted as visible blue.
Some highlighted phrases may span two lines, but a pen circle or underline must
stay a short unbroken span. If the mark doesn't fit, shorten the marked phrase,
not the educational copy.

### Do not misuse the devices

- Do not put both a box and a circle around the same phrase.
- An underline affirms a point; a strike-through rejects one. Don't strike out a
  valid operational state such as sent or booked.
- Draw circles with two uneven strokes, not a perfectly geometric ellipse.
- Never combine the existing .pay and .sel rules: the text color can go wrong.
- Keep each selected phrase in its sentence; do not reorder copy to place a box.
- A blue box can highlight a template field, not pretend to be an interactive UI.
- A highlight cannot carry a critical meaning absent from the text itself.

## 6. Hierarchy, spacing and named shared layouts

Use the fixed 1080 × 1350 page, 96px side margins and 24px vertical grid. Preserve
clear bands for the eyebrow, content and footer. Supporting copy needs room, but
the page doesn't need to be filled from top to bottom.

The old template's 96px title and 62px running explanation suit short statements,
not every instructional page. Implement the following named teaching-page tokens
in the shared stylesheet before rendering. Do not silently shrink type per page.

| Role | Proposed size / leading | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cover title | 96px / 120px | Strong deliverable, without a giant six-line title. |
| Teaching title | 78px / 96px | Clear lesson above usable explanation. |
| Teaching body | 48px / 72px | About 16px when a 1080px page is shown at 360px width. |
| Field or row heading | 54px / 72px | Scanable worksheet labels and short comparison rows. |
| Scope/footer note and eyebrow | 36px / 48px | About 12px at 360px width; reserve up to two footer lines, never shrink to fit. |
| Payoff word, if the sentence supports one | Existing Zodiak 192px | One word, not a whole paragraph in a second face. |

These tokens are implemented in the shared teaching stylesheet and used by the
two generated decks. A teaching page needs a visible title/body difference; it should not
look like two paragraphs set in the same weight and size.

Budget a 48px title-to-body gap. Use 24px spacing within a related group and 48–72px
between different groups. Structured comparisons and lists should use existing
rules rather than shiny cards, drop shadows or fake application windows.

If a page oversets, adjust the composition or split the idea within the 7–11-page
range. Don't reduce body type below the documented teaching size. Keep the closing
byline separate from useful copy and don't put annotation over the footer rule.

### Layout library

| Named composition | Content treatment |
| --- | --- |
| cover | Short benefit-led title, enough setup to state the deliverable, one blue selection and one gold mark. |
| reference | Title above a reference/evidence line and a short instruction; align labels and values, no simulated UI. |
| statement | Claim, supporting explanation and one named action; keep the paragraph out of oversized display type. |
| contrast | Two clearly labeled states or definitions, followed by the check that distinguishes them; keep text order unchanged. |
| checklist | Two or three short ruled rows drawn from the body, plus the practical check; no invented numbered steps. |
| roles | Separate source/processing/human responsibilities with spacing and rules; do not draw an AI decision box. |
| exception | Trigger or missing item, named owner and next action; a visible failure route, not decorative arrows. |
| boundary | What the evidence confirms versus what it doesn't; keep scope in main text as well as the footer. |
| template | Set the written field names as a legible reusable list; the action sentence sits underneath. No fake app frame. |
| evidence | Keep source, basis and value context together. Use annotations to connect the check, not to imply verification. |
| payoff | One authority or completion boundary emphasized on the dark page; supporting instruction stays readable. |
| worked-example | Example label, the stated values or test cases, then the interpretation. No fake report or authoritative result. |
| close | Summarize the method and give one practical action; no generic follow/comment/download gate. |

The layout names are editorial treatments, not an instruction to build 13 unrelated
visual themes. Start with three shared structures: lesson, structured evidence/list,
and comparison/template. Add a variant only if the content needs it.

HTML reading order must remain title, body and footer so the renderer's exact-copy
validation and PDF extraction still match the canonical writing. Splitting a body
into list rows must not add fake numbers or omit connecting words.

## 8. Review at mobile scale and full resolution

Before accepting the next proof, inspect the full PDF pages and a 360px-wide preview.
The body must be readable without zooming; scope must not depend on an illegible
footer. Keep provider/test/proposed boundaries in the copy where the claim needs them.

Check contrast for navy on cream, navy on gold and cream on the blue box. Use at
least 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text. Annotation color must not be the
only way to understand a status or human-review boundary.

Check that blue appears as substantive marks, not just page numbers. Look for
adjacent pages dominated by repeated gold swipes. Check that the one dark page
emphasizes an important boundary rather than making the deck's last page dark by habit.

## 9. Honest acceptance criteria

Content review passes only if all six documents deliver their promised checklist,
method or worksheet, each page adds a distinct point, and arithmetic/source
interpretation is correct. A long deck with repeated slogans fails.

Visual review passes only after the new source is rendered and checked. Do not
claim that a written plan has solved fit, color balance or mobile legibility.
Compare against vendor decks and both rejected proofs; record remaining weaknesses.

Technical review checks exact copy, page count, mark fit, content/footer clearance,
loaded fonts, equal page dimensions, embedding and the single shared grain tile.
Those tests do not establish that the user likes the design.
