## 2. What the content should establish

The author builds custom business automation. This leasing demo is evidence of implementation judgment, not a SaaS launch, screening bureau or finished client deployment.

The central position: handle repetitive leasing coordination and prepare reviewable evidence without letting AI choose who gets a home. Here's the distinction. The build should make that division visible, not hide it in a disclaimer.

Primary readers are US residential PM owners and operations leaders; leasing leads and application reviewers are important secondary readers. The 50–500-home range is an initial project-planning hypothesis, not a validated customer cutoff. Content should speak to repeated work and missing handoffs, not assume a portfolio size proves need.

### What the reader should leave believing

- Routine coordination and rental judgment are different kinds of work.
- This builder has tested an actual synthetic flow, not only drawn an architecture.
- A production version would start from the company's existing systems and policies.
- Important actions need evidence, permissions and an accountable human route.
- A useful conversation starts with a specific process gap, not buying AI for its own sake.

### What we aren't claiming

No client time savings, occupancy lift, fraud-detection rate, production reliability percentage or approved testimonial exists for this build. Don't manufacture one. The blueprint's scale examples are hypothetical, and passing a set of checks isn't a model accuracy study.

Pricing, deployment timing, guarantees and recurring support terms remain unapproved. The closing post invites a process conversation; it doesn't introduce a discount, free audit or fixed implementation promise.

### Editorial length targets

The full demo edit targets about 6–9 minutes, allowing more time if that's necessary to show the real handoffs clearly. Don't speed through the manager review to hit a number. Remove dead time, not the evidence.

B5-08 targets 45–60 seconds. Supporting text posts generally target 150–250 words; carousel captions target 80–140 words. These are writing targets, not proven optimal lengths or platform limits.

Each carousel carries one idea per slide, usually about 20–35 words of main copy, with a short source or boundary note where needed. Mobile legibility takes priority over filling the page. Dense legal explanations belong in the source context, not tiny slide footnotes.

### Why these formats

The full video shows the work. The short isolates one decision-sensitive handoff. Carousels explain distinctions a reader can retain, and text posts report a specific lesson or address a practical objection.

No format is assigned a guaranteed reach advantage. This is an editorial choice. Evaluate what attracts the intended readers after publication instead of importing generic algorithm claims.
